Why Use the Library?

You already have Google. Here's what you're missing without the library.

🔎 Google gives you...

  • Results ranked by popularity, not quality
  • Ads, sponsored content, and paywalls
  • No guarantee of accuracy or expertise

📚 The library gives you...

  • Expert-reviewed, quality-controlled sources
  • Free full-text access to expensive databases
  • A librarian who can help you find exactly what you need
💡 Your tuition already pays for it. Tulane students have free access to hundreds of millions of dollars worth of research databases, journals, and books. Not using them is like buying a meal plan and only eating the breadsticks.

Building Your Search: Terms & Boolean Operators

Step 1 — Break your question into concepts

Example question: "How does social media use affect depression among college students?"

Concepts: social media · depression · college students

Then brainstorm synonyms for each: social media → Instagram, TikTok, online platforms · depression → anxiety, mental health · college students → undergraduates, young adults

Step 2 — Combine concepts with Boolean operators

# AND — narrows results (both terms must appear) social media AND depression AND "college students" # OR — broadens results (use for synonyms) depression OR anxiety OR "mental health" # NOT — excludes a term "social media" NOT marketing

"Quotation marks"

Search an exact phrase: "college students", "social media addiction". Without quotes, databases treat each word separately.

Truncation (*)

depress* finds: depression, depressed, depressive.
teen* finds: teen, teens, teenager. The asterisk is a wildcard for word endings.

# Putting it all together: ("social media" OR Instagram OR TikTok) AND (depress* OR anxi* OR "mental health") AND ("college students" OR undergrad*)
💡 Found a good article? Look at its keywords and subject headings — that's your next set of search terms.

🔧 Troubleshooting Your Search Results

Searching is iterative — you'll almost never get perfect results on the first try. Here's how to adjust.

Too many results? ▼
Add more concepts with AND, use quotation marks for exact phrases, add date filters, or limit to peer-reviewed sources. The more specific you are, the more relevant your results.
Too few results? ▼
Use OR to add synonyms, try truncation (depress* instead of depression), remove one concept, or try a broader database like Academic Search Complete.
Results aren't relevant? ▼
Try searching "Subject" instead of "All Text". Use NOT to exclude irrelevant topics. Look at the subject headings of a good article and use those terms in your next search.
Can't access full text? ▼
Look for "Full Text @ Tulane" or "FindIt@Tulane" links. Try searching the title in Library Search. As a last resort, use Interlibrary Loan — it's free and articles arrive in 1–3 days!
Not sure which database to use? ▼
Start with your subject's Research Guide at libguides.tulane.edu — each guide lists the best databases for that field. When in doubt, Academic Search Complete covers most disciplines. You can also browse all 900+ databases by subject.
Found a great article — how do I find more like it? ▼
Check the article's references for earlier sources and use "Cited by" features (available in Google Scholar and Web of Science) to find newer articles that build on it. Also look at the article's subject headings/keywords and use those in your next search.
💡 Remember: A "failed" search isn't wasted — it tells you what to adjust. Change one thing at a time (add a synonym, remove a concept, try a different database) so you can see what works.

Summary & Next Steps

Five Things to Remember

1
Scholarly ≠ Credible. All scholarly sources are credible, but not all credible sources are scholarly.
2
Break your topic into concepts, then add synonyms. AND to narrow, OR to broaden, NOT to exclude.
3
Use the right tool. Google for background. Library Search for broad. Subject databases for depth.
4
Full text is almost always available. FindIt@Tulane, Google Scholar linked, or ILL.
5
Librarians are research partners. Chat for quick help. Consultation for big projects.
