Why Use the Library?

You already have Google. Here's what you're missing without the library.

🔎 Google gives you...

  • Results ranked by popularity, not quality
  • Ads, sponsored content, and paywalls
  • No guarantee of accuracy or expertise

📚 The library gives you...

  • Expert-reviewed, quality-controlled sources
  • Free full-text access to expensive databases
  • A librarian who can help you find exactly what you need
💡 Your tuition already pays for it. Tulane students have free access to hundreds of millions of dollars worth of research databases, journals, and books. Not using them is like buying a meal plan and only eating the breadsticks.

Building Your Search: Terms & Boolean Operators

Step 1 — Break your question into concepts

Example question: "How does social media use affect depression among college students?"

Concepts:  social media  ·  depression  ·  college students

Then brainstorm synonyms for each: social media → Instagram, TikTok, online platforms  ·  depression → anxiety, mental health  ·  college students → undergraduates, young adults

Step 2 — Combine concepts with Boolean operators

# AND — narrows results (both terms must appear) social media AND depression AND "college students" # OR — broadens results (use for synonyms) depression OR anxiety OR "mental health" # NOT — excludes a term "social media" NOT marketing

"Quotation marks"

Search an exact phrase: "college students", "social media addiction". Without quotes, databases treat each word separately.

Truncation (*)

depress* finds: depression, depressed, depressive.
teen* finds: teen, teens, teenager. The asterisk is a wildcard for word endings.

# Putting it all together: ("social media" OR Instagram OR TikTok) AND (depress* OR anxi* OR "mental health") AND ("college students" OR undergrad*)
💡 Found a good article? Look at its keywords and subject headings — that's your next set of search terms.

The Search Ecosystem: Choosing the Right Tool

A library database is a curated, searchable collection of scholarly content with filters for date, peer-review status, subject, and more. Unlike Google, every item in a database has been cataloged and quality-checked.

🎓 Google Scholar + Tulane

Link Google Scholar to Tulane once and you'll see "Full Text @ Tulane" buttons instead of $30–50 paywalls.

One-time setup:

  1. Go to scholar.google.com
  2. Menu (☰) → Settings → Library links
  3. Search Tulane → check Full Text @ Tulane → Save

✅ Done once. Works every time you use Google Scholar after that.

🔧 Troubleshooting Your Search Results

Too many results? ▼
Add more concepts with AND, use quotation marks for exact phrases, add date filters, or limit to peer-reviewed sources.
Too few results? ▼
Use OR to add synonyms, try truncation (depress* instead of depression), remove one concept, or try a broader database like Academic Search Complete.
Results aren't relevant? ▼
Try searching "Subject" instead of "All Text". Use NOT to exclude irrelevant topics. Look at subject headings of a good article and use those terms.
Can't access full text? ▼
Look for "Full Text @ Tulane" or "FindIt@Tulane" links. Try searching the title in Library Search. As a last resort, use Interlibrary Loan — it's free!

Interlibrary Loan (ILL)

Can't find a source in Tulane's collection? ILL lets you request it from another library — anywhere in the world — for free.

🔗 Submit a request: library.tulane.edu/services/interlibrary-loan  ·  Log in with your Tulane credentials and fill in the title, author, and DOI if you have it.

📄 Articles & Book Chapters

  • Delivered as a PDF to your email
  • Typical turnaround: 1–3 days

📚 Books & Physical Materials

  • Pick up at the library
  • Typical turnaround: 5–10 days
💡 Plan ahead. ILL is fast — but not instant. Submit requests early in your research process, not the night before something is due.

Getting Help

Librarians support every stage of the research process — not just when you're lost. Here's how to reach us and when each option works best.

💬 Chat

Instant help from the library website — no appointment needed.

Use for: Quick questions, "where do I find X?", database access issues

📧 Email

Reach your subject librarian directly.

Use for: Follow-up on a consultation, questions that need a detailed answer, checking in on an ILL request

📅 Research Consultation

A dedicated one-on-one session with a subject librarian.

Use for: Developing a search strategy, thesis or capstone research, a topic you've never researched before

💬 Discussion & Reflection

Before we wrap up, let's push the thinking a bit further. These questions don't have right answers — they're worth sitting with.

🔭 Discussion Prompts

1. Google vs. the library. Given what you learned today, when would you still start with Google — and when would you go straight to a database? What's your decision-making process?

2. Is peer-reviewed always best? The peer-review process ensures quality — but it's also slow and has historically excluded certain voices. When might a credible non-scholarly source actually be more useful?

3. Whose knowledge gets found? Your search terms shape what research you find — and what you don't. If you only search the terms most common in your field, whose perspectives might you be missing?

4. Your research right now. Think about a project you're working on or have coming up. What's the single most useful thing from today that you'll actually apply? What's still the hardest part?

Summary & Next Steps

Five Things to Remember

1
Scholarly ≠ Credible. All scholarly sources are credible, but not all credible sources are scholarly. Check your rubric.
2
Break your topic into concepts, then add synonyms. Use AND to narrow, OR to broaden, NOT to exclude. Quotes lock in phrases; * expands word endings.
3
Use the right tool for the job. Google for background. Library Search to explore broadly. Subject databases for in-depth disciplinary research.
4
Full text is almost always available. FindIt@Tulane, Google Scholar with Tulane linked, or ILL — you almost never need to pay for an article.
5
Librarians are research partners, not just lookup desks. Chat for quick help. Schedule a consultation for bigger projects.
